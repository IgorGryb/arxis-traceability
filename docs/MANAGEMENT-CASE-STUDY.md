# ARXIS — management case study

Private review draft. Design study, not a client deployment.

## Problem

A changed requirement can leave inconsistent decisions, versions and deliverables in circulation.

## Approach

An operating model for traceable design documentation and controlled project change.

The design connects source evidence, explicit assumptions, operating ownership and model checks. Its primary output is a decision package that can be handed to an execution team.

## Key decision

Use one synthetic residential case and document handoffs before adding integrations or AI beyond assistance.

## Operating design

1. Register requirements and authority
2. Map requirements to decisions and deliverables
3. Assign version and review owner
4. Check traceability and resolve conflicts
5. Release baseline and propagate change

## Economics and validation

Estimate coordination and rework cost from hours per change × loaded rate. Avoided rework is only a scenario until measured in a real project; no construction liability or certified-design claim.

Testable hypothesis: Explicit ownership and dependency mapping reduce uncontrolled version drift.

Negative check: Delete a decision link or change a requirement; release gate must fail and identify affected artifacts.

## Outcome and limits

The current result is a documented management model with explicit handoffs, metrics and validation cases. Historical technical prototypes are supporting evidence only. Market adoption, customer impact and production operation are not established.

Traceability completeness does not prove correctness of engineering design or compliance with building law.

## Next decision

Walk through a cross-discipline change and test whether each handoff preserves approval authority.
